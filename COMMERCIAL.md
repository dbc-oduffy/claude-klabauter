# Commercial Use

Claude-klabauter is **source-available**: Apache 2.0 with a Commons Clause rider. The rider
is the only thing that separates this from a plain Apache 2.0 grant, and it is narrow.

## What the standard grant already covers — no permission needed

- **Personal use** — running the engine on your own projects.
- **Internal commercial use** — running it inside a company, on company work, to build and
  ship company products. Using the engine *at work* is fine and always has been.
- **Modification** — adapting, extending, forking, renaming, replacing whole subsystems.
- **Redistribution of derivative works**, under the same terms.
- **Contribution** — sending improvements back.

## What the rider bars

A separate commercial licence is required to **Sell the Software** — that is, to provide to
third parties, for a fee, a product or service whose value derives entirely or substantially
from this engine's functionality. Concretely:

- **Repackaging** the engine (or substantial portions) as a commercial product.
- **Reselling** this orchestration engine or a thin derivative as a paid offering.
- **Bundling** it into a commercial tool or hosted service sold to others.

Selling *your own product* that happens to use the engine internally is not this. Selling
*the engine* is.

## Free written internal-use grant — available on request

The Commons Clause makes this licence non-OSI, and plenty of larger organisations run a
blanket policy against non-OSI licences. That policy is usually a procurement default rather
than a considered objection to the rider, and it would auto-reject this project in exactly
the places adoption is most wanted.

So: **a free, perpetual, written internal-use grant is available on request, at no cost and
with no negotiation.** It grants unrestricted internal use, modification, and internal
redistribution — everything above under "the standard grant" — in a signed document your
legal or procurement team can file. It does not grant the right to Sell the Software; that
is still the paid channel below.

To request one, [open a GitHub
issue](https://github.com/dbc-example-operator/claude-klabauter/issues) with the label
`internal-use-grant`, stating:

1. The organisation name the grant should be issued to.
2. A contact for the countersigned document.

That is the whole process. No commercial conversation attaches to it, and asking does not
put you on a sales track. If the licence text is the only thing between your organisation
and using this engine, this exists precisely to remove it.

## Paid commercial redistribution licence

For the resale cases the rider bars, [open a GitHub
issue](https://github.com/dbc-example-operator/claude-klabauter/issues) with the label
`commercial-license`, or contact Dónal example-operator directly.

## Why this model

The engine is free to use and free to modify. The rider exists only so that if someone
builds a business on this work, the original author is not cut out of it. If you are unsure
whether your use case needs a commercial licence, it almost certainly does not — but ask,
and you will get a straight answer.
